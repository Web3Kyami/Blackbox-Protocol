## A) Decision verdict

**Agree with B as the next architectural direction, but not with B exactly as specified.** Option A cannot satisfy the invariant that final scoring is contract-owned or contract-verified. Option C is the likely long-term model, but it materially increases custody, adapter, oracle, accounting, and audit risk. B offers the best mainnet-MVP trade-off only after its accounting model is tightened.

Security and product trade-offs:

- **A:** Fastest and most capital-efficient, but the proposed fraud proof is undefined. A bond does not make arbitrary off-chain wealth provable, and the existing action escrow is not directly reusable as a slashable registration bond. A remains trust-based and should not be the mainnet answer.
- **B:** Low new custody risk, simpler UX, and substantially less implementation risk than C. It supports a credible narrow product: strategies managing a single liquid settlement token and fully liquidating by close.
- **C:** Strongest control over holdings, but share accounting alone does not value external positions; it still needs trusted adapters and usually oracles. “1–2 weeks” is optimistic for an audited vault holding real value.
- **Stepping stone:** B’s measured settlement and checkpoint interfaces can carry forward, but the accounting core will not. Moving from wallet balances to vault shares is a significant rewrite, so “compatible stepping stone” is fair; “clean migration path” is overstated.

### Holes inside B

As written, B is **not yet economically sound**:

1. **Uncontrolled inflows make `balance_of` gameable.** A wallet can receive gifts, loans, mint proceeds, or short-term liquidity immediately before close. The contract cannot inspect ERC-20 transfer-event history or determine whether a balance increase is trading profit. The claim that unexplained jumps are enforceably forfeitable is unsupported by the proposed mechanism.

2. **Flash/close manipulation remains possible.** An account or callback-capable protocol can temporarily increase the wallet balance, call `close()` while the funds are present, then unwind. Storing the snapshot makes the false value final; not storing it makes the winner mutable after close.

3. **Existing escrows distort B’s balance.** Escrowed bonds remain in the Arena until after close, so a wallet-level final balance excludes its outstanding bonds. B must include each registrant’s Arena-held escrow in NAV, or change the lifecycle. Keeping escrow “exactly as built” would systematically penalize users who posted bonds.

4. **A single-token balance measures cash, not portfolio value.** Off-token assets and liabilities are invisible. B therefore requires forced liquidation by close, but a normal ERC-20 wallet cannot prove that it holds no other positions or debts.

5. **Checkpoint count is not coverage.** Permissionless callers can produce N checkpoints in one block or time window. Require scheduled windows, minimum spacing, one accepted snapshot per window, and an explicit missing-window rule. Checkpoints also need bounded storage and gas behavior.

6. **Close scalability:** reading an external token balance for every candidate compounds the existing unbounded winner loop. Finalization needs a registration cap, economically meaningful registration bond, or batched/finalized snapshots.

7. **Token risk:** the float token must have pinned semantics—decimals, non-rebasing behavior, no public mint, predictable `balance_of`, and acceptable upgrade/blacklist controls. The rehearsal TestUSD is openly mintable, so it cannot demonstrate B’s adversarial integrity.

8. **Snapshot semantics:** close must atomically persist every final value or a final winner. `settle()` must never reread mutable wallet balances.

My recommendation is therefore **B′: single-token, forced-liquidation competition with flow-adjusted NAV**. At minimum:

`NAV = wallet balance + Arena-held refundable escrow − tracked external contributions`

All permitted capital contributions/withdrawals must pass through contract-accounted paths. If ordinary wallet transfers remain unrestricted, B can honestly claim “contract-observed token balance,” but not “verified strategy return.”

## B) Project assessment

### Architecture

The architecture has good bones: registrant binding, an immutable adapter assignment, deterministic integer scoring, explicit receipt replay protection, permissionless lifecycle transitions, raw-u256 escrow custody, and separation of on-chain state from indexer/web views.

However, the live class is not mainnet-safe. The current final score reads caller-supplied `current_value` and `drawdown_bps` ([arena.cairo](/root/projects/BlackBox%20Arena/contracts/src/arena.cairo:427)), directly contradicting the final-scoring invariant in [AGENTS.md](/root/projects/BlackBox%20Arena/AGENTS.md:16). The documentation acknowledges this, which is honest, but it remains a release blocker.

### Contract security posture

Material findings:

- **Critical – permanent close DoS through score overflow.** `portfolio_value_after` accepts any `u128`, but scoring unwraps it into `i128`, then unwraps return BPS into `i64` ([arena.cairo](/root/projects/BlackBox%20Arena/contracts/src/arena.cairo:692)). A registrant can submit an oversized final value, causing every `close()` to panic after the round has ended, with no recovery path.

- **High – unbounded registration/finalization.** Registration is free and uncapped, while `get_winner()` loops over every registration ([arena.cairo](/root/projects/BlackBox%20Arena/contracts/src/arena.cairo:716)). A Sybil can make close exceed transaction limits. B would add an external `balance_of` call per entry and worsen this.

- **High – prize denomination mismatch in rehearsal.** The script treats one TestUSD as `10^18` raw units for floats and escrows, but deposits and pays `100` raw units ([honest-round.mjs](/root/projects/BlackBox%20Arena/scripts/honest-round.mjs:244)). Thus the verified payout is 100 base units—`10^-16` TestUSD under the script’s 18-decimal convention—not 100 whole TestUSD. The transfer is exact, but the human-readable claim is wrong.

- **Medium/high – settlement interaction ordering.** `settle()` calls the external prize token before setting `settled` and settlement state ([arena.cairo](/root/projects/BlackBox%20Arena/contracts/src/arena.cairo:661)). With the rehearsed standard token this is not presently exploitable, but it is callback/reentrancy-unsafe for a malicious or nonstandard configured token. Use checks-effects-interactions or a guard.

- **Medium – custody pools are not segregated.** Prize funds and action escrows may be the same token and share one balance. Settlement checks total Arena balance rather than prize-reserved balance. A fee-on-transfer or malicious prize token could make accounting insolvent and allow escrow funds to mask a prize shortfall.

- **Medium – mutable rules surface.** Sponsor-controlled assets and targets can be added after round start ([arena.cairo](/root/projects/BlackBox%20Arena/contracts/src/arena.cairo:354)). The rules hash is caller-supplied rather than recomputed by the contract. These should be frozen before registration/start.

- **Medium – stranded funds.** Over-cap prize deposits intentionally remain in the Arena, but there is no excess-prize recovery. Deposits are also allowed after settlement.

Escrow math itself is improved: expected raw amount uses u256 multiplication, actual balance delta must match, raw custody is stored, and refunds zero state before transfer. Integer BPS truncation matches the declared rule. Missing tests include arithmetic extremes, malicious/callback tokens, balance segregation, registration gas limits, target mutation after start, prize decimals, score overflow, and close liveness.

### Verification methodology

The rehearsal methodology is substantially better than typical demo evidence:

- Fail-closed writes.
- Receipt status and event provenance.
- Same-address view assertions.
- Historical balance replays.
- Independent score recomputation.
- Exact raw escrow/refund agreement.
- Distinct strategist wallets and permissionless callers.

But its conclusions are narrower than the prose:

- The “real trade” is a plain ERC-20 transfer to the dummy address `0x123456789`, not a venue trade ([honest-round.mjs](/root/projects/BlackBox%20Arena/scripts/honest-round.mjs:279)). The scoring action then calls `open_submit_action` directly, not the bound adapter. Therefore v4 proves transfers, reporting, escrow, and lifecycle—not real adapter-mediated trading on Sepolia.
- Historical “pre” balance uses block `n-1`, not transaction pre-state; that assumption breaks if relevant transactions share a block ([open-round-crosscheck.mjs](/root/projects/BlackBox%20Arena/scripts/open-round-crosscheck.mjs:91)).
- Event matching does not consistently verify event selectors.
- Winner crosscheck is specialized to two candidates and does not reproduce the complete tie-break.
- The rules commitment in this rehearsal hashes only start/end times, despite other parameters affecting the round ([honest-round.mjs](/root/projects/BlackBox%20Arena/scripts/honest-round.mjs:157)).
- `getClassAt` establishes code presence but does not compare the deployed class hash to the claimed class.
- Accepting `PENDING` as successful is too weak for final evidence.

## C) What remains for mainnet

The repository correctly lists value-axis enforcement, external review, public-RPC dashboard work, and funded operations/monitoring ([STATUS.md](/root/projects/BlackBox%20Arena/docs/STATUS.md:313)). I would expand the gate to:

1. Specify and threat-model B′, including inflow/outflow accounting, escrow-inclusive NAV, forced liquidation, checkpoint windows, final snapshot storage, token requirements, and exact raw-unit conventions.
2. Fix close-liveness issues: arithmetic bounds, registration cap/economic bond, bounded or batched finalization.
3. Fix prize decimals, reserve segregation, excess recovery, and settlement interaction ordering.
4. Freeze all scoring-relevant configuration before registration and make the rules commitment cover every actual parameter.
5. Replace the dummy target with audited mainnet venue adapters and run a Sepolia v5 involving genuine adapter execution.
6. Add adversarial Cairo tests, fuzz/property tests, and token-behavior tests before external audit.
7. Obtain an external audit covering Arena, adapters, token assumptions, prize/escrow accounting, and B’s economic manipulation model.
8. Move the dashboard to public RPC/indexer reads with RPC redundancy, chain/class/address verification, wallet-only participant writes, and failure-closed UI behavior.
9. Establish mainnet governance and operations: multisig sponsor, least-privilege roles, deployment reproducibility/source verification, fee budget, monitoring, checkpoint/close/refund keepers, incident response, and upgrade/migration policy.
10. Publish precise claims: shielding remains `UNVERIFIED` on Sepolia; wallet/token/amount visibility must be disclosed; rehearsal transfers must not be called real venue trades.

Items missed by both repo lists are principally **close DoS, prize-unit correctness, escrow-inclusive B accounting, token/reentrancy assumptions, registration economics, rules freezing, and stranded/excess-fund recovery**.

## D) Red flags

- Current final scoring violates the stated contract-owned/verified invariant through both self-reported value and self-reported drawdown.
- The escrowed path does not update portfolio value, despite its interface comment saying value derives from observed escrow; it only verifies the bond/allocation.
- “FALCON paid 100 TestUSD” is misleading: the script verifies a payment of 100 raw units.
- “Real trades” and “adapter actions” overstate v4: the script transfers to a dummy sink and submits directly to the Arena.
- “This is the last architectural question between us and mainnet” is too strong given the independent contract-level blockers above.
- “No griefing vector” in the `close()` comment is false while registration is unbounded and score conversion can be deliberately made to panic.
- B’s statement that balance jumps can be classified as unexplained and forfeited is not implementable from plain `balance_of` checkpoints alone.
- B’s estimated 1–2 days is reasonable for a prototype, not for a manipulation-resistant mainnet design and verification cycle.

Static review only; no tests were run because the requested mode prohibited filesystem changes. The worktree remained clean.

**Overall verdict: B′ is the right next step, but the current class and the proposed B specification are not mainnet-ready, and several claims in the v4 evidence materially overstate what was proved.**
