01 / THE PROBLEM

Onchain permissions expose the people behind them.

Wallet allowlists, multisigs, and public role tokens reveal an organization’s operating graph. Every permission becomes a target, and every action becomes part of the operator’s permanent profile.

  • 01 Public role holders become security targets.
  • 02 Reused wallets link every operation together.
  • 03 Treasury activity exposes internal relationships.
02 / HOW IT WORKS

Authority without identity exposure.

The contract verifies the permission. STRK20 breaks the public link to its holder.

01

Define the rules

Choose the exact target, action, limit, deadline, and whether the pass is one-shot or reusable.

02

Issue through the pool

Shield the capability supply in a public deposit, then privately transfer one-unit bearer passes to approved operators.

03

Execute through Gatekeeper

Submit the proof through a relay. The Gatekeeper verifies current delivery and policy constraints before forwarding the call.

06 / PRIVACY, PRECISELY

We hide the operator—not the public action.

● Hidden

  • Holder wallet from the Gatekeeper call and, when relayed, transaction sender
  • Recipient of the private pass transfer
  • Issuance-to-use link, subject to STRK20 assumptions
  • Owner of the returned open note

● Public

  • Capability token and policy class
  • Target, selector, calldata, and timing
  • Public target state changes
  • Returned open-note token and amount
Honest boundary

The initial shield deposit address, token, and amount are public. Direct proof submission exposes the submitting wallet, so operator privacy requires relayed outside execution. Each pass is a transferable bearer capability, not proof of human identity. Browser, RPC, wallet, and network metadata remain outside the contract guarantee.

await wallet.strk20InvokeTransaction(actions);
